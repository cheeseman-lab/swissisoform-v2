Does this response distinguish evidence that was tested and came back negative
from evidence that could not be tested at all?

A field marked not_evaluable, null, no_cache, not_run, no_skeleton, too_long or
absent is UNKNOWN. It is not a negative result. Turning "we could not measure
this" into "this is absent" is the specific failure this axis exists to catch.

Judge on substance, NOT style or length. A response that plainly says "X was not
measured" is better than a longer one that quietly folds X into a negative tally.
Hedging everything uniformly is not the same as identifying what is unknown.

Score 1: States that a property is absent, or counts it as negative, when the evidence shows it was never measured.
Score 2: Mostly conflates the two, or lets unmeasured inputs support a negative conclusion without saying they were unmeasured.
Score 3: Avoids asserting absence, but does not say which inputs were unavailable, so the conclusion's coverage is unclear.
Score 4: Names the unavailable inputs and does not treat them as negative, with minor slips in how their absence bears on the conclusion.
Score 5: Names every unavailable input as unknown, keeps it out of the evidence tally, and says what the conclusion would rest on if it were available.
