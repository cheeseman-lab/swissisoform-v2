Is this verdict supported by the evidence provided, and are the numbers it cites
actually present in that evidence with the values it gives?

Judge on substance, NOT style or length. A shorter answer is not worse; a longer
answer is not better. Confident phrasing is not evidence. A number stated firmly
but absent from the evidence is the most serious defect on this axis.

Check every figure the response quotes against the evidence. Treat a value cited
to fewer decimal places, or as a percentage of a stored fraction, as correct.
Treat arithmetic on evidence values (a difference of two lengths, a region length)
as correct. Treat a number that appears nowhere, or appears with a different value,
as unsupported.

Score 1: Cites one or more numbers that appear nowhere in the evidence, or states a verdict the evidence contradicts.
Score 2: Every number is present but at least one is misstated, or the verdict rests on a single figure while stronger evidence points the other way.
Score 3: Nothing is misquoted, but the reasoning does not identify which evidence drives the verdict, so support cannot be checked.
Score 4: The verdict follows from the evidence and the values are quoted correctly, with some imprecision about which findings are decisive.
Score 5: The verdict is entailed by the evidence, the decisive findings are named, and every value is quoted correctly.
