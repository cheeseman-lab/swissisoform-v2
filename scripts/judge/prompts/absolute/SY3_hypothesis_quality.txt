Is the divergence hypothesis specific enough to be wrong — does it say what
changed, and what would distinguish it from the obvious alternative?

Judge on substance, NOT style or length. A one-sentence hypothesis naming a
concrete consequence beats a paragraph of qualified possibility. "May affect
function" is not a hypothesis.

The test is falsifiability against THIS isoform: could the same text be pasted
onto a different isoform in the set without becoming wrong? If so it is generic.

Score 1: No hypothesis, or one so generic it would fit any isoform in the set.
Score 2: Names a change but no mechanism or consequence that anything could test.
Score 3: A plausible, isoform-specific hypothesis, with no indication of what would confirm or refute it.
Score 4: A specific hypothesis tied to this isoform's evidence, with a partial sense of what would test it.
Score 5: A specific, falsifiable hypothesis tied to the evidence, naming what would distinguish it from the obvious alternative.
