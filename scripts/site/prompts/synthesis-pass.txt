Your job is an INTERACTION ANALYSIS, not a summary. The pipeline has PREDICTED or QUERIED a set of findings for this isoform (localization, domains, conservation, variants, structure, etc.). Separately, the GENE has an ESTABLISHED biology from the literature. Your task: cross-examine the predicted/queried findings against that known biology and say how they INTERACT — which finding CONFIRMS, CONFLICTS WITH, EXTENDS, or has NO BEARING on what the protein is already known to do — then lead with the one or two interactions that genuinely reshape or contradict the established function. Do NOT walk the six categories one by one or restate their verdicts — that laundry list is explicitly not wanted. Start from the known biology; end at "so this finding changes / contradicts / sharpens X about it."

Read the 16 findings as THREE TIERS, and treat them very differently in your output:
  1. IS IT REAL? — Conservation (C1/C2/C3) and Detection (D1/D2/D3). These say the isoform is a genuine, translated ORF. They are EXISTENCE evidence, not a functional change. REASON over them to set `confidence`, but NEVER read them out and NEVER tag them.

WHAT MECHANISM CHANGES? — Localization (L1/L2), Domains (S1), Structure/fold (P1 structured extension, P2 core refold, P3 secondary-structure element gained or lost in the differential region), Biophysics (S2), interpretable features (S3). These are the ONLY findings computed DIFFERENTIALLY (isoform vs canonical), so they are the ONLY things that can state how the isoform is functionally UNIQUE from the canonical. This tier — contrasted against the known biology — is the SOLE source of your readout (headline / divergence_hypothesis / function_relevance) and of the tags.
  3. DOES IT MATTER? — Mutation Landscape (M1 germline constraint, M2 disease-variant overlap), and Conservation again. These say the differential region COINCIDES with an important site. REASONED OVER, NOT READ OUT: tiers 1 and 3 (conservation, detection, variant density, germline constraint) must NOT appear in `headline` / `divergence_hypothesis` / `function_relevance`. Calibration rule (weigh a prediction's baseline read before trusting its isoform read):
  - A prediction is only trustworthy for THIS protein if it reads the CANONICAL/baseline in line with the known biology. But if `predicted_canonical` ALREADY disagrees with `known_from_literature` (e.g. literature says nucleus, DeepLoc calls the canonical ER), DeepLoc is off for this protein — a differing isoform call is NOT evidence of relocalization; down-weight it, do not tag.

Controlled tag vocabulary (assign only when a confident, calibration-passing finding interacts with the KNOWN biology — each is one axis of functional interaction):

  - "Domain gain" — a real InterPro domain is gained in the differential region (typically an extension).
  - "Truncated functional region" — a truncation removes an N-terminal region that is structured, conserved, or domain-bearing (functionally important to the known biology, not just sequence).

  - "Core refold" — the retained (shared) region is predicted to fold differently, AND the structure confidence supports it (pTM and shared-region pLDDT high). Do NOT assign on RMSD alone when confidence is low.
  - "Interpretable-feature shift" — differential ESM-C SAE features fire (S3 scored true).
